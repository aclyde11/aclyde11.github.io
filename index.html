About

I design interpretable AI for high-performance scientific systems and study how algorithmic decisions reshape civic life. My work spans large language models for drug discovery and biological design, AI calibration, and the democratic and human-rights implications of automated systems. I am twice recognized by ACM with the Gordon Bell Special Prize (2020, 2022) and contributed to the National Virtual Biotechnology Laboratory's COVID-19 response, honored with a U.S. Department of Energy Secretary's Award.

Research focus

How we build and interpret AI for science — and how society interprets AI in return.

Explainability

Sample-based explainability for scientific AI: linking model behavior to data choices, experimental noise, and interdisciplinary collaboration.

Scrutiny

Practical techniques to interrogate LLMs and edge models, treating them as knowledge systems that must be audited before deployment.

Calibration

Automatic calibration for large-scale AI workflows and scientific campaigns, including resilient calibration for LLMs.

Law, Democracy & Rights

Research on civic epistemologies, legal interpretation of AI systems, and how transparency standards shape democratic participation.
